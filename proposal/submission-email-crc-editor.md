The working title is:

**Blockchain-Based Identity and Provenance for Distributed Energy Assets**  
*Immutable Identity, Lifecycle Traceability, and Integrity Verification for Solar and Renewable Infrastructure*

The book addresses a practical problem I have encountered in renewable-energy work: photovoltaic modules, batteries, and related hardware are traded and regulated on the basis of records that are not reliably bound to the physical assets they describe. With digital product passport requirements now taking effect for batteries in the European Union and broader traceability pressure across solar supply chains, I believe there is a need for a careful engineering treatment of how verifiable identity can be designed for distributed energy hardware — without the promotional tone that has often surrounded blockchain applications in the energy sector.

The manuscript is a single-author monograph of approximately **116,000 words** (fourteen chapters, two appendices). It is written for graduate students, researchers, and practising engineers.
